Information Security and Cyber Law

       

 8.1 Digital Society and Computer Ethics

A digital society** is a society where computers, the internet and other digital technologies are widely used in daily life. People use digital technology for communication, education, banking, shopping, entertainment, business and government services. Examples of digital society include online classes, online banking, e-commerce, social media and digital payment systems.

Computer ethics** refers to the moral principles and rules that guide people to use computers and information technology properly and responsibly. A computer user should respect the privacy of others, avoid unauthorized access to information, not copy software illegally, not spread false information and not damage computer systems. Computer ethics helps create a safe, responsible and trustworthy digital society.

8.2 Concept of Information Security

Information security** is the process of protecting information and information systems from unauthorized access, use, modification, disclosure, damage or destruction. Information security is important because personal, financial, educational and business information can be misused if it is not properly protected. The main goals of information security are **confidentiality, integrity and availability**, which are known as the **CIA Triad**. Confidentiality means that information should only be accessed by authorized users. Integrity means that information should remain accurate and should not be changed without permission. Availability means that authorized users should be able to access information whenever it is needed.

 8.3 Concept of Cybercrime

cybercrime** is an illegal activity performed using computers, computer networks, the internet or other digital technologies. Cybercriminals may try to steal information, damage computer systems, commit fraud or gain unauthorized access to accounts and networks. Common examples of cybercrime include hacking, identity theft, online fraud, cyberbullying, software piracy and data theft. Cybercrime can cause financial loss, privacy problems and damage to individuals, organizations and society.

8.4 Malicious Software and Spam

Malicious software**, commonly called **malware**, is software designed to damage a computer system, steal information or perform unauthorized activities. Common types of malware include **virus, worm, Trojan horse, spyware and ransomware**. A virus can attach itself to files and spread when those files are used. A worm can spread automatically through networks. A Trojan horse appears to be a useful or legitimate program but performs harmful activities. Spyware secretly monitors activities or collects information, while ransomware can prevent users from accessing their data and demand payment. **Spam** refers to unwanted and unsolicited electronic messages, especially emails. Spam may contain advertisements, misleading information or harmful links.

 8.5 Protection from Cybercrime

Cybercrime can be reduced by following proper security practices. Users should create **strong and unique passwords** and should avoid sharing them with others. Antivirus software can be used to detect and remove malware, while a **firewall** can help prevent unauthorized network access. Operating systems and applications should be regularly updated because updates often fix security weaknesses. Important files should be backed up regularly so that they can be recovered if they are lost or damaged. Users should also avoid clicking suspicious links or opening unknown attachments. **Encryption** can be used to protect sensitive information by converting readable data into a coded form that unauthorized people cannot easily understand.

#8.6 Intellectual Property Right

**Intellectual Property Right (IPR)** refers to the legal rights given to creators and owners of original creations, inventions and ideas. It protects people from the unauthorized use, copying or distribution of their intellectual work. The major types of intellectual property include **copyright, patent and trademark**. Copyright protects original creative works such as books, music, photographs, films and computer software. A patent provides legal protection to a new invention or technical innovation. A trademark protects names, logos, symbols and other identifiers used to distinguish a product or organization. Intellectual property rights encourage creativity and innovation by giving creators legal protection over their work.

 8.7 Digital Signature

A **digital signature** is an electronic method used to verify the authenticity and integrity of a digital document, message or transaction. It uses cryptographic techniques to confirm that the document was created or approved by the claimed sender and that it has not been changed improperly. Digital signatures are useful in online transactions, electronic documents, e-government services and electronic agreements. They provide authentication, help maintain data integrity and make digital transactions more secure.

#8.8 Cyber Law in Nepal

**Cyber law** refers to the laws and legal rules that control activities involving computers, the internet, networks and digital information. Cyber law helps prevent and control illegal activities in cyberspace and provides legal protection for digital users and organizations. In Nepal, the **Electronic Transactions Act, 2063 (2006)** is an important law related to electronic transactions and cyber activities. It provides a legal framework for electronic records, digital signatures and various cyber-related offences. Cyber law is important for creating a secure and reliable environment for using information and communication technology.

 8.9 ICT Policy in Nepal

**ICT Policy** refers to government policies and strategies developed to promote the proper use and development of Information and Communication Technology in Nepal. ICT policies aim to expand digital services, improve ICT infrastructure, increase digital literacy and promote the use of technology in areas such as education, health, business and government. They also support e-governance, the development of the IT sector and wider access to information and communication technologies. Effective ICT policies help Nepal move towards a more digitally developed and connected society.
